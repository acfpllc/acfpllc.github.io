Cities we serve

From the head office in Elgin, ACFP LLC manages HOAs, townhome communities, and condominium associations in Chicago and 13 suburbs. The list below follows the official areas we serve page. Each city heading links to ACFP’s longer local page for that town. Facts such as ZIP codes, routes, and landmarks are taken from those pages. If your community sits between two of these cities, ACFP most likely covers it too.

Illinois association law and local permits

ACFP’s city pages note that association obligations depend on the community’s legal structure, governing documents, and applicable law. Management supports administration; association counsel should address legal interpretations and disputes. The statutes commonly cited on those pages are:

Illinois Condominium Property Act

765 ILCS 605, which addresses condominium governance, including budgets, assessments, records, and board responsibilities.

Common Interest Community Association Act

765 ILCS 160, which applies to covered non-condominium associations. Exemptions exist, so boards should confirm applicability rather than assume every HOA or townhome community falls under the same provisions.

Community Association Manager Licensing

The Community Association Manager Licensing and Disciplinary Act, 225 ILCS 427, establishes Illinois licensing requirements. Boards should verify applicable manager and firm licenses through IDFPR when evaluating providers.

Village or city building permits are separate from association architectural approval. Confirm both before work begins. Permit departments for each city are named on that city’s official ACFP page.

Frequently asked questions

Which cities does ACFP list as service areas?

ACFP has dedicated pages for Chicago, Elgin, Naperville, Aurora, Schaumburg, Joliet, Arlington Heights, Evanston, St. Charles, Crystal Lake, Wheaton, Plainfield, Waukegan, and Downers Grove. Those 14 city pages are not the edge of the map; ACFP also takes on associations in neighboring towns across the Chicago suburbs.

Which counties are covered?

The listed cities sit in Cook, Kane, DuPage, Will, McHenry, and Lake counties. Aurora and Plainfield pages also note Kendall County.

Do you manage associations outside Illinois?

Outside Illinois, ACFP’s remote HOA management supports self-managed associations in most U.S. states, with a few exceptions. Boards can ask ACFP to confirm whether a specific state is eligible.

How does a board change management companies?

The usual path is to request a proposal (city, number of units, and association type), meet the team about current problems and priorities, review a written proposal that shows scope and fees, then transition records, bank accounts, and vendor contracts and set up owner portal access. Timing depends on the current contract, notice requirements, board approvals, and the transfer of records.

Is there a charge to request a proposal?

No. Proposal requests are complimentary and carry no obligation. Most proposals are delivered within a few business days after the first follow-up. Start on the request a proposal page.
